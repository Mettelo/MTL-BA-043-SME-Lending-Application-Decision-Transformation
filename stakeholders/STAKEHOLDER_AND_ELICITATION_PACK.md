# Stakeholder and elicitation source pack
**Mettelo BA-043 · Stage 2 · Interviews 8–10 July; workshop 13 July**
All dialogue is synthetic. Roles provide source organisational context, not a completed influence assessment, engagement plan or RACI.

## Organisation and authority
COO Daniel Reed sponsors operations change. Olivia James is a relationship manager reporting to Sales. Marcus Lee is senior underwriter. Farah Ahmed owns Credit Risk policy/delegation. Clare Wilson leads Compliance. Peter Grant manages intake Operations. Oliver King leads integration. Sophie White manages Finance/funding reconciliation. Ravi Desai is the fictional director of Birch Tools Ltd, linked to A0551. Each can validate a different part of the journey; none supplies blanket approval.

## 1. Olivia James — relationship manager
Olivia's target is viable applications and funded business. She wants customers to get a useful answer quickly and is concerned that the project will add fields without reducing duplicated chasing.

**Interviewer:** What does complete mean?
**Olivia:** “Enough to discuss a deal. If I have a pack from the customer I mark it complete. Underwriting uses verified complete differently. We should not pretend my status means all checks are finished, but customers do not know the internal distinction.”

For A0551 she describes accounts supplied in stages and repeated requests. She cannot establish from the source whether the same document was unusable, missing from the folder or simply not seen. She asks the BA to separate those explanations instead of counting every repeated request as an Operations error.

**Interviewer:** Why ask for automatic decisions?
**Olivia:** “A small, straightforward company should not wait behind a complex case. I said below £75,000 because that is the underwriter limit I remember. That does not mean a value threshold proves a decision is straightforward. Farah needs to tell us what is authorised.”

She wants a clear next action and a customer-visible explanation of what is missing. She also wants fewer mandatory fields and the ability to save incomplete work. When asked about evidence and audit, she acknowledges the need to retain what was relied on and not simply overwrite earlier statements.

**Interviewer:** Is approved in principle useful?
**Olivia:** “Commercially yes, but I can see how a customer hears it as certainty. We need agreed language. I cannot promise funding before the required checks and decision.”

Unresolved: Olivia has no representative lost-business or applicant-effort study and cannot approve risk policy, regulatory perimeter or vendor data use. She offers one session and asks that customer-sector differences and the relationship manager's informal work are not lost in a system-only design.

## 2. Marcus Lee — senior underwriter
Marcus assesses evidence and prepares/records decisions within delegation. He is measured on timeliness and decision quality. He worries that a fast workflow will be celebrated while verification and audit work remain hidden.

**Interviewer:** Why return documents?
**Marcus:** “An attachment tick is not enough. Accounts may omit a period, a bank file may be stale or a company record may not match. A document can be present but not suitable. Equally, I have received an email copy that never entered CaseVault and a colleague asks for it again.”

A0553 has a recorded company/bureau mismatch and no verified-complete date by cutoff. Marcus says a general awaiting-credit status is unhelpful because a person must resolve the facts. The dataset does not include a real bureau result or a verified explanation of the mismatch.

**Interviewer:** Can a score make the decision?
**Marcus:** “Not under our present authority. A score might support work, but somebody must know the evidence, exception and policy. A nominal human click is not the same as meaningful review. I will not sign an automation claim based on easy fabricated cases.”

He wants an attributable rationale and policy version, but admits some current reasoning remains in workbook notes or email. He asks the BA to distinguish missing retained evidence from an assertion that no reasoning happened. He cannot reconstruct absent notes for the exercise.

**Interviewer:** What should be tested?
**Marcus:** “A file that changes after completion, an exception below the normal cash limit, a withdrawal, a repeat document and a mismatched identity. A happy path is not sufficient. But I need the BA's validated needs, not a supplier's pass list.”

Unresolved: complete touch time, exact repeated-check prevalence and all exception decisions are unavailable. Marcus can validate process evidence and proposed test expectations within his role; he cannot waive Credit Risk approval or certify a model.

## 3. Farah Ahmed — Credit Risk Manager
Farah owns the fictional policy, appetite and delegated authority. She supports better evidence flow but expects risk and decision ownership to remain explicit.

**Interviewer:** What is the existing limit?
**Farah:** “Under current v4, standard/no-exception cases up to and including £75,000 can be decided by a senior underwriter; up to £100,000 by Credit Manager; above that to £150,000 by Committee. Exceptions need Credit Manager review at any amount, and Committee above £100,000. A March slide says £100,000 for the senior role. That slide is not a signed replacement policy.”

She explains that amount, exception status and applicable policy version are different facts. A0555 at £75,000 is a useful boundary example under the recorded June policy, but it does not become an approved automated rule. A later policy change must state effective date and treatment of existing/materially changed cases.

**Interviewer:** Are missing rationale references a credit failure?
**Farah:** “They are an evidence concern. We should investigate where the rationale exists and whether authority was appropriate. Do not turn a blank export field into a conclusion that the loan is bad. Equally, do not excuse unavailable evidence because a colleague says they remember the reasoning.”

She wants versioned evidence, role/approver attribution and a way to identify unresolved decisions. She does not approve indefinite retention or access for every team. Compliance and information owners must participate.

**Interviewer:** Would you support digital workflow?
**Farah:** “If it makes ownership and evidence better. I need options, control implications and independent acceptance. I do not need a system that copies the old slide into an automatic threshold.”

Unresolved: no approved credit model, model validation, complete audit population or funded-loan outcomes are supplied. Farah can review proposed business-rule interpretations but cannot endorse all vendor data terms or sponsor funding. She expects the BA to make policy uncertainty visible and preserve traceability when decisions change.

## 4. Clare Wilson — Compliance Manager
Clare reviews applicability, customer communications, supplier handling and oversight. She is concerned that a pilot will expand the product without noticing the changed obligations.

**Interviewer:** Why be precise about applicants?
**Clare:** “This is a limited-company product pilot. Do not add sole traders or partnerships by changing a dropdown and call it the same scope. Identify the product/customer perimeter and ask the relevant applicability questions. A general statement about SME finance is not a legal conclusion about every case.”

She notes that the FCA contextual statement supplied for teaching was published after the fictional July appointment. It can inform current learning but must not be backdated as evidence available to the sponsor.

**Interviewer:** What about vendor automation?
**Clare:** “First establish what decision is made, on what data, by whom and with what oversight. A human sign-off box does not establish actual review. Vendor reuse for unrelated training is not assumed permission. I need the proposed purpose, transfer, access, retention and contractual arrangements.”

Clare wants understandable communications separating indicative discussion, final decision and funding. She asks whether withdrawn applicants stop receiving reminders and whether evidence remains appropriately controlled. She does not provide a ready-made retention schedule or require collection of every possible document.

**Interviewer:** What approval can you give today?
**Clare:** “A list of issues and the route for focused review. Not a blanket regulatory approval of a platform or a case outcome. Bring specific assumptions, source facts and proposed handling.”

Unresolved: vendor detailed terms, approved retention, complete customer research and the applicability review for any proposed expansion remain absent. Clare can prioritise one focused review weekly; late retrospective review may affect the desired pilot. The BA should cost and schedule it rather than treat control work as an optional appendix.

## 5. Peter Grant — Operations Manager
Peter owns intake queues and document chasing. His nine colleagues also handle status calls and withdrawals. He is concerned that each department uses a local complete/awaiting label that hides the next owner.

**Interviewer:** Why are requests repeated?
**Peter:** “Sometimes the customer sent the wrong period. Sometimes the relationship manager has an attachment in email but it is not in the case folder. Sometimes a colleague repeats a request because no receipt is visible. We need evidence before blaming one team.”

A0552 arrived through more than one intake conversation. Operations reconciled it into one analysis application. Peter says that does not prove the live systems prevent duplicate rows. He wants a controlled link/merge decision with evidence rather than deleting one record and losing history.

**Interviewer:** What is the difficult status?
**Peter:** “Awaiting credit can mean waiting for us, the customer, verification or an approver. An applicant rings and gets a different answer depending on which file is open. I want the next action and owner, not just a brighter label.”

He asks for automatic reminders, then recognises that A0556's withdrawal should suppress inappropriate chasing. A generic reminder schedule could increase complaint demand if status is stale. He has not defined every exception or approved the communications.

**Interviewer:** How would success be measured?
**Peter:** “Count the work we create as well as what leaves the queue. A completed-only average might improve while pending applications wait longer. Less chasing can release capacity, but that is not automatically cash saving.”

Unresolved: no full time study, complete duplicate population or authoritative ownership model is available. Peter can support observation and a limited replay but not a staffing assumption that removes people before the work is demonstrably reduced. He wants transition treatment for existing applications, not a clean new-case-only demo.

## 6. Ravi Desai — fictional applicant, Birch Tools Ltd / A0551
Ravi is a company director seeking £60,000 for the business. This synthetic account is not representative research or a real loan decision.

**Interviewer:** What was difficult?
**Ravi:** “I uploaded accounts, then received another request. I could not tell whether the file was missing, the period was wrong or a different team had not seen it. A checklist of what is actually needed would help, but I also need someone who can explain an unusual situation.”

A0551 was received 1 June, recorded verified-complete 12 June and approved 17 June. Ravi's customer account covers the entire interval; the lender's complete-file clock excludes the earlier period. The pack does not include all original uploads or the evidence underlying the approval.

**Interviewer:** Would an instant answer help?
**Ravi:** “A reliable one. If approved in principle sounds like final approval and changes later, speed is not the only thing that matters. Tell me what is provisional and what I can rely on. A bank transaction that happens later is another stage.”

He wants to save work, know what has been received and avoid unnecessary repeated uploads. He does not propose removing appropriate checks. He is concerned about commercially sensitive information being visible to people who do not need it or reused by a supplier for unrelated purposes.

**Interviewer:** What about automated decline?
**Ravi:** “I would want an understandable explanation of the process and a way to raise incorrect information. I cannot specify your credit policy, but I do not want an error in a company record silently deciding everything.”

Unresolved: this account cannot quantify conversion loss, willingness to pay or representative accessibility. No funding outcome is supplied. Further research should include different company sizes, sectors, digital confidence and experience of declined/withdrawn applications, with appropriate synthetic or governed data.

## 7. Oliver King — IT and integration lead
Oliver supports WebForm, CaseVault and interfaces to existing bureau/funding products. He wants coherent identity and audit, not another parallel tracker.

**Interviewer:** Must systems be replaced?
**Oliver:** “Not established. We may improve existing worklists, uploads and evidence references. That still needs development, access review, migration/reconciliation and support. The supplier's demo used fabricated data and assumed interfaces we have not contracted.”

He notes that a customer upload receipt proves an upload event, not that the document passed verification. A company ID, application ID and bureau reference are not interchangeable. Resubmission or document replacement needs a retained relation and appropriate policy interpretation, not a silent overwrite.

**Interviewer:** What should the BA clarify?
**Oliver:** “Meaning, ownership and consequences of delay. What happens if a bureau file fails, a document changes after completion or two sources disagree? If the need is faster decision, which step requires immediate data and what can wait? Generic real time is not a complete requirement.”

He has no approved model-validation evidence, new data source or complete audit export. Existing licences do not prove the current product can meet every need; a current-tools option must be tested too.

Unresolved: interface scope/capacity, final access model, recovery, retention and resource allocation are absent. September infrastructure work competes for the same specialist. Oliver can provide indicative effort, not a guaranteed launch from a product catalogue.

## 8. Sophie White — Finance and funding reconciliation
Sophie owns the handover from authorised decision to funding records. She wants costs and benefits to be separated from requested loan values.

**Interviewer:** What do the amounts show?
**Sophie:** “Requested principal is not revenue, funding or profit. Approved is not drawn. The dataset cannot establish arrears or whether an automated decision would improve risk. Please do not turn £26.22 million of June requests into a benefit figure.”

She asks that the £150,000 envelope include the £15,000 already committed and that annual costs include support, verification charges and licences where incremental. Opportunity cost and backfill need separate assumptions. Staff capacity released is not automatically a reduction in payroll.

**Interviewer:** What worries you in the audit?
**Sophie:** “A funding handover needs evidence of the authorised decision and the right company/application. If a rationale is in an email nobody can retrieve, reconciliation and review are harder. But a blank reference does not prove a loan default or an unauthorised payment.”

She wants a staged approach costed honestly and rejects selecting the cheapest quoted setup without exclusions. She will not approve a funding or control bypass simply because Sales wants an instant answer.

Unresolved: no actual funding dataset, attributable loss model, complete supplier charge schedule or approved savings mechanism is supplied. Sophie expects the BA to show decision-quality improvements and evaluation ownership, including what cannot yet be monetised.
